Attendance and participation

Regular attendance and active participation in class. If you have to miss a class, let me know beforehand.

Random notes

Post six observations of things related to the course topic that surprised you in the Moodle discussion forum. A few sentences each is enough: what you noticed, where, and why it surprised you. Spread them over the semester (at least three by reading week) so that we can discuss them in class.

Metaphor data

In the metaphor week we collect PERSON is ANIMAL metaphors across languages in a shared sheet. Each student must check at least 30 rows in the tab for their language: fill in or correct the translation and mark the row Y or N (or leave it blank with a comment if you are unsure). You can work in pairs, in which case check at least 30 rows each. This is not graded, but it must be done, and the data feeds into the class discussion and into a research project.

Presentation on a minority language

Choose one minority language (or dialect of a major language) in the study area and prepare a short presentation of at most 10 slides. Suggested content and a list of possible languages are on the week 10 page. Upload your slides to Moodle the day before.

Presentation of seminar work

  • Presentations can be done in a group of at most 3 students or individually.
  • The topic is free, but it must be approved before week 11 (see the milestones).
  • You have 10 minutes: 7 for the presentation, 3 for questions.
  • Upload your slides to Moodle one day before the presentation.
  • Formatting
    • Make sure your names are on the slides.
    • Add page numbers to each slide (except optionally the first one).

Seminar paper

  • Seminar papers can be written in a group of at most 3 students (normally the same group as the presentation) or individually.
  • Normally the paper is on the same topic as your presentation; if you really want to do something else, email me.
  • Each student studies one grammatical or linguistic phenomenon in a language or languages of the area and writes about it, showing both knowledge of the specialist literature and the ability to analyse and describe the phenomenon. The paper can be an overview (research), original research, creative, etc.
  • An individual paper should be at least 5 pages long. Group work should be proportionally more extensive: 5-6 pages for one student, 7-8 pages for two, 9-10 pages for three, not including references.
  • The submission deadline is {{ due }} (end of day, anywhere in the world). Submit a PDF via Moodle.
  • Formatting
    • 5-10 A4 pages, single spaced, with the title, date and your name(s) on the first page (no separate title page).
    • Include a Contributions section at the end (after the conclusion, before the references) that says which author contributed what.
    • If you want to make it even more beautiful, as I am sure you do, take a look at my (Computational) Linguistic Style Guidelines: a guide for the flummoxed.
