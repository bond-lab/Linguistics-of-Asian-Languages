Metaphor across Languages

Slides: Metaphors across Languages

Please look at some examples of PERSON is ANIMAL metaphors for your language before the next lesson. You can work in pairs (or triples).

The data is here

  • First choose the tab for your language (Japanese, Czech, Slovak or Korean). If you want to do a new language, copy the English tab and rename it.
  • Each row is one metaphor: an English animal word and the meaning it has when used for a person. The Source column says where the row comes from:
    • omw-en: a sense pair from the English WordNet
    • otherwise a metaphor that students added in 2024 or 2025, e.g. Slovak 2025: čajka [Y] means the Slovak students found that čajka 'seagull' can mean a young woman. Please check whether the same metaphor works in your language.
  • The Language column is already filled in where we had a translation: either from previous students of your language, or automatically from a lexicon (then the Comment starts with auto:). Please correct it if it is wrong, and fill it in if it is empty.
  • The Previous column shows what earlier students of your language decided (2025: N). You can disagree with them! (default) means the row was probably never checked.
  • For each row decide whether the metaphor can be used in your language, with the translation you have written:
    • If it can, mark it with Y
      E.g., for Japanese 狐 'fox' can also mean a shifty deceptive person so 'Y'
    • If not, mark it with N
      E.g., for Japanese 狐 'fox' can not mean attractive person so 'N'
    • If you are not sure, leave it blank and say why in the Comment
    • If the animal word has another metaphorical meaning for a person, add it in a new row directly below, with a definition of its meaning, and mark it Y.
      E.g., for Japanese 犬 'dog' can mean 'snitch; informer; informant; spy' so add this and mark it 'Y'
    • If there are other animal metaphors you know about, feel free to add them at the end. For example, in Korean, 청개구리 cheong-gaeguri "green tree frog/disobedient person" (Explanation here)
      In this case add definitions for the literal meaning and the metaphorical meaning, and put the English name of the animal in the English column.
  • Please do not delete rows or change the Source, Previous, Animal sense or Person Sense columns: we use them to compare languages and years.
  • We will then discuss the similarities and differences in class.
